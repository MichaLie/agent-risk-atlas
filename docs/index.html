How to read it

Columns follow the research loop; a risk sits at the step where the failure arises. Rows say who fails. Many are old failures that agents make faster and quieter. Click a risk to see the human failure that often lets it through (dark ring and dashed line) and the defences against it; click a defence to see what it covers; click empty space or press Esc to reset; arrow keys step through the risks. A ? marks an open question: nothing here convinces the author yet. Clicking a risk or a defence also shows an example you could adapt for your agent's instructions. For a ten-minute narrative introduction aimed at researchers and PhD students, read The Quiet Errors Inside Good-Looking Science; a visual companion page also traces seven concrete errors into the paper and five capability tracks into deskilling as you switch defence groups on.

Example instructions

Examples, in plain words, of what to ask your agent. Adapt them; none has been tested. Most ask the agent to show you something, because a rule in a file does not prove it was followed (E4). Two habits help all of them: ask the agent to put its questions together, and to wait for your answer when it asks for your prediction, choice or brief.
